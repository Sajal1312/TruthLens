Purpose: understand how people read evidence-assessment presentations. You will
see randomly assigned presentations and answer questions about clarity,
usefulness, limitations and your understanding. We are evaluating the
presentation, not testing you. Some passages are clearly marked as constructed
examples. Outputs can be incomplete or mistaken and must not be treated as
authoritative fact checks. The precise comparison will be explained afterward.

Planned task: 12 short reading tasks, estimated 25–40 minutes; the time estimate
has not been measured. Participation is voluntary. You may skip any question,
take a break or stop without penalty. Participation must not affect grades or
your relationship with the investigator. Possible burdens are reading fatigue
or mild uncertainty. No direct benefit or compensation is promised.

Data: your assigned anonymous code, optional ratings, quiz answers, answer
confidence, optional comments and facilitator-measured reading/task time if
agreed. Do not enter names, emails or identifying details in comments. No
automatic submission, network tracking, IP logging or device profiling occurs
in the supplied offline form. Clicking download creates a local JSON file;
you choose whether to give it to the organizer. Source links are ordinary web
links; opening them later contacts those websites outside this offline form.

request removal by code. After aggregation/de-identification, removal may no

identities, if the institution requires them, must be stored separately.
